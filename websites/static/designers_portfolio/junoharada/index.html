001 — Identity P. 014 / 015

Meridian
Press

Client
Meridian Press, London
Year
2023
Role
Identity, type, art direction
Output
Logo system, books, stationery

The brief asked for "modern classic." I told them the brief was a contradiction. We built it anyway. The mark is a horizon cut through a serif M — the press's initial — set in a custom cut of Halcyon drawn specifically so the M could be cleanly bisected without losing its skeleton.

The system runs on three weights, two paper stocks, and one rule: nothing centred. The press's editors resisted for two weeks, then admitted the left-aligned catalogue was the first object they'd seen that looked like the books they wanted to publish.

"A logo is a sentence compressed to a letter. Most designers stop at the letter."

002 — Type Design P. 028 / 029

Halcyon
Typeface

Client
Self-initiated, via Press
Year
2022
Role
Drawing, spacing, production
Output
8 weights, 2 italics, variable

Halcyon began as a problem: I needed a serif that could hold a 280pt headline and a 9pt footnote without redrawing. Most type families fudge this — the display cut is a different drawing from the text. I wanted one curve, scaled.

The result took fourteen months and four abandoned versions. The italic is not a slanted roman; it leans on a six-degree axis with a separately drawn stress. The descenders are longer than they should be. Designers keep telling me this. I know.

"A typeface is a hundred decisions pretending to be one."
Fig. 2.1 — Halcyon Display Italic, opsz 144
003 — Packaging P. 042 / 043

Kassia
Records

Client
Kassia Records, Berlin
Year
2021
Role
Packaging, identity, type
Output
LP sleeves, labels, inserts

A record sleeve is a 312mm square. Most designers treat this as a constraint. Kassia and I treated it as a format — like a sonnet, or a haiku. The system is identical across releases: one weight of Halcyon, one position for the catalogue number, one rule for the artist's name.

What changes is the back. Each release carries a full-bleed photograph of the room the record was made in. The label said this was sentimental. I said people buy records to feel like they were there. The first pressing sold out in eleven days.

"A format is not a limitation. It is a contract with the reader."

004 — Posters P. 056 / 057

The Long
Now

Client
The Long Now Foundation
Year
2020
Role
Poster system, type
Output
12 posters, programme

Twelve lectures, twelve posters, one question: how do you design for an organisation that thinks in ten-thousand-year increments? The answer was to design something that would look the same in five hundred years — which is to say, no decorative elements, no current technology, just type on paper.

Each poster carries the lecture title in Halcyon at 180pt, the date in JetBrains Mono, and nothing else. No illustrations, no photographs. The series was acquired by MoMA's permanent collection in 2022, which I mention only because they paid more for the posters than the Long Now paid to commission them.

"If you can remove an element and the work still reads, the element was decoration."
Fig. 4.1 — System mark, with timeline
005 — Identity P. 070 / 071

Forme
& Press

Client
Forme & Press, Bristol
Year
2019
Role
Identity, custom lettering
Output
Logo, business papers, signage

A letterpress studio run by two former architects. They wanted a logo "with a serif but not too serif." We discussed this for an afternoon. What they meant, it turned out, was that they wanted the ampersand to do the heavy lifting.

I drew an ampersand that referenced the construction of Caslon but with the terminal of a claw hammer — a small joke about their architect's hands. The wordmark is set in lower case because, after eight rounds, they admitted upper case felt too official for a studio with a cat called Maurice.

"The ampersand is the most honest letter. It admits there is more than one thing."
