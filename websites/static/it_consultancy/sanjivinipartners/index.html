01 — Services

Five ways we get involved.

Most engagements start with one and grow into two or three. You don't have to know which you need — that's the first conversation.

Out of the box, Zoho is powerful and generic. We configure CRM, Books, Inventory, People and Creator around how your pipeline actually runs, connect the apps to each other, and take you live in phases with training that sticks.

Discuss this service
  • Zoho One rollout with governance & cross-app workflows
  • Custom functions & integrations — Tally, gateways, logistics
  • Phased go-live with role-based training

Most operational pain isn't a missing tool — it's a handoff nobody owns. We map the work with the people doing it, find the real bottleneck, and give you a roadmap sequenced by impact, not by vendor.

Discuss this service
  • Process discovery & mapping, on site
  • Bottleneck & cost analysis
  • Build-vs-configure recommendation & roadmap

When configuration runs out, we build software: customer and vendor portals, internal operations tools, and native Android apps — designed to behave on patchy connectivity and integrated with the systems you already run.

Discuss this service
  • Customer & vendor portals with real access control
  • Native Android applications
  • API design & integration into existing systems

Multi-vendor platforms fail in unglamorous places — seller onboarding, catalogue, delivery slots, settlement. We've shipped one end to end and operate it every day, so we know exactly where they break.

Discuss this service
  • Seller onboarding & verification
  • Catalogue, pricing & availability operations
  • Delivery zones, slots & settlement logic

The cheapest wins are usually the processes people do by habit — follow-ups, sheets, approvals. We automate them with workflow tools and low-code, in days rather than quarters.

Discuss this service
  • Approvals & escalations that route themselves
  • Zoho Flow, Deluge, AppSheet & Apps Script
  • Reporting that assembles itself overnight

Consulting usually comes first — it decides which of the other four follow. You don't have to know which you need; that's the first conversation.
