I got into software the way you get into an open world — no map, no quest markers, just curiosity and a stubborn streak. What began with view-source and broken tutorials became a discipline: take messy, real problems and ship systems that survive contact with users.

These days I work the full vertical. React and Next.js at the surface, Node and Postgres underneath, ML pipelines wherever they genuinely earn their keep — and, when nobody's watching, compilers and virtual machines in Rust, because I like knowing what the machine is actually doing.

Two years architecting Zoho CRM automations for real businesses taught me the thing I value most: software is only good if it still runs on a Friday evening, when nobody is babysitting it. I build to that standard — and then I document it, so the next person doesn't have to guess.
